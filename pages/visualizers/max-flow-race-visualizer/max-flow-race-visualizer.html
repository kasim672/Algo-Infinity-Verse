⚔️ The Algorithms

🔍

Edmonds-Karp (Ford-Fulkerson)

Maintains a residual graph. In each iteration, it runs a full Breadth-First Search (BFS) to find the shortest augmenting path from Source to Sink. It then pushes the maximum possible flow along this entire path. It is O(V E^2).

🌊

Push-Relabel

Maintains a "preflow" where nodes can have excess flow. Nodes have a "height". Flow is pushed greedily to lower neighbors. If stuck, the node increases its height (relabel). It works entirely locally, achieving O(V^2 E) or better.

💥

Adversarial Graphs

On bipartite graphs with many small cross edges, Edmonds-Karp is forced to do many full BFS sweeps, pushing tiny amounts of flow each time. Push-Relabel simply pools the flow and pushes it across locally, vastly outperforming EK.
