Chinese Remainder Theorem

Reconstructing a Number from its Shadows

The Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) proves that if you know a number's remainder when divided by several coprime moduli, you can uniquely reconstruct the original number! This elegant math is the engine behind RSA's rapid decryption speed and Shamir's Distributed Secret Sharing.

Real-World Application: RSA-CRT Speedup

In standard RSA, decryption requires computing $M = C^d \pmod{N}$. Because $N$ and $d$ are massive (e.g. 2048 bits), this is very slow.

Since the owner knows the prime factors $p$ and $q$ (where $N = p \times q$), they use the Chinese Remainder Theorem:

Direct Exponentiation

$M = C^d \pmod{N}$
Time Complexity: $O(\text{bits}^3)$
VS

CRT Optimization

$M_p = C^{d \pmod{p-1}} \pmod p$
$M_q = C^{d \pmod{q-1}} \pmod q$
$M = \text{CRT}(M_p, M_q, p, q)$
Time Complexity: $\approx 4\times$ Faster!

By breaking the giant modulus $N$ into two smaller moduli $p$ and $q$, the numbers being exponentiated are half the size. Since exponentiation cost scales cubically with bit length, $(1/2)^3 + (1/2)^3 = 1/4$, making decryption roughly 4 times faster! This is why real-world keys are stored in a format containing $d_p$, $d_q$, and $q_{inv}$.
