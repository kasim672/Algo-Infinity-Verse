Haar Wavelet Transform

Time meets Frequency. The math behind JPEG2000.

Unlike the Fourier Transform which loses time information, Wavelets decompose signals into both Frequency and Position simultaneously. By recursively splitting a signal into "Averages" (Approximation) and "Differences" (Detail), we can isolate noise, compress data, and analyze multi-resolution features perfectly!

✨ How It Works

➗

Decomposition

At each step, we look at adjacent pairs of numbers. We replace them with their Average (the smooth approximation) and their Half-Difference (the sharp detail). We repeat this process recursively only on the Averages.

🔍

Multi-Resolution

Level 1 details represent high frequencies (rapid changes). Level 2 details represent medium frequencies, and so on. The final Average is the DC component (the global average of the entire signal).

🧩

Reconstruction

The transform is completely lossless. To go backwards, simply use $Avg + Diff = Left$ and $Avg - Diff = Right$. By repeating this from the coarsest level back to level 0, you restore the original signal perfectly.

🗜️

Compression Magic

Real-world signals are mostly smooth, meaning most Detail coefficients are close to zero. By thresholding (forcing small details to 0), we can throw away most of the array and still reconstruct a highly accurate approximation. This powers JPEG2000!
